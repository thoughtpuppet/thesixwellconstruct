Making as inquiry.

As an Atlanta native, Saiel Dauhn Solehman’s work emerges from the merging of deep philosophical inquiry and systems thinking. Her practice begins with fundamental questions about meaning, perception, truth, memory, interpretation, identity... the structures that shape human experience. Rather than treating these questions as isolated ideas, she examines the larger systems around them: culture, language, environments, and the relationships between people.

This approach allows her to move between mediums without treating them as separate practices. Painting, tattooing, clothing, built objects, media, events, and creative systems become different ways of investigating how meaning is formed, communicated, processed, implemented, organized, and transformed.

Her work is less concerned with producing singular answers than with tracing relationships: between the individual and the collective, thought and structure, symbol and interpretation, intention and consequence. Her approach is grounded in an effort to understand the world rather than change it. In this way, making becomes a form of inquiry. The object, image, environment, or system is not only the result of the question, but part of the process of thinking through it.

Solehman currently studies philosophy, politics, and economics at Georgia State University, using her academic work to deepen and better inform the practice she has already established. Her studies provide additional language, historical context, and analytical frameworks for examining the questions and systems that continue to surface throughout her work.
