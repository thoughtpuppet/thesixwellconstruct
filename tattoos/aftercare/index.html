Aftercare Instructions

A practical guide for protecting your new tattoo while your skin heals.

Follow the covering and product instructions given at your appointment first. Contact the studio if those instructions are unclear or your covering fails.

First priority

Keep it clean. Keep it gentle.

A fresh tattoo is healing skin. Wash your hands before touching it, avoid friction, and do not pick, scratch, or peel it.

The first days

Clean, dry, and lightly moisturized.

Covering

Follow your appointment instructions

Keep the original covering on for the time specified at your appointment. Wash your hands before removing it. Do not re-bandage the tattoo unless the studio directs you to.

Washing

Use clean hands and lukewarm water

Gently wash with a mild, fragrance-free cleanser. Do not scrub or use a washcloth. Rinse thoroughly, then pat dry with a clean paper towel.

Moisture

Apply only a thin layer

Use the fragrance-free moisturizer or ointment recommended at your appointment. Apply a light layer; the tattoo should not stay greasy, wet, or smothered.

Clothing

Prevent sticking and friction

Wear clean, loose, breathable clothing over the area. Avoid tight fabric, repeated rubbing, and anything that can stick to or contaminate the tattoo.

While it heals

Let the skin complete its process.

  • Expected changes: Mild soreness, redness, swelling, clear fluid, itching, flaking, and light scabbing can occur as a tattoo heals.
  • Do not pick: Let flakes and scabs release naturally. Picking or scratching can injure the skin, remove pigment, and increase infection risk.
  • Avoid soaking: Showering is fine, but do not submerge the tattoo in baths, pools, hot tubs, lakes, or other shared water until it is fully healed.
  • Avoid direct sun: Keep a healing tattoo out of direct sunlight. After it is fully healed, use broad-spectrum sunscreen to help protect the skin and pigment.
  • Keep products simple: Avoid exfoliants, fragranced products, and unapproved treatments on the tattoo while it heals.
Medical attention

Know when healing is no longer routine.

Contact a doctor or board-certified dermatologist promptly if redness becomes darker or spreads, pain or swelling worsens instead of improving, the skin feels increasingly hot, or you develop pus, significant drainage, odor, open sores, red streaks, fever, chills, or shivering.

Seek emergency care for trouble breathing, chest tightness, dizziness, hives, intense swelling, or another serious whole-body reaction. Tell the studio what is happening, but do not rely on a tattoo artist to diagnose or treat a medical problem.

Health references

This general guide is informed by dermatologist-reviewed guidance from the Cleveland Clinic and the American Academy of Dermatology. It does not replace individualized medical advice.
