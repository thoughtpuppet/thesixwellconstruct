What You Should Know Before Booking

Open each subject before choosing a path. This is the complete proposed guide, not a teaser to another page.

How requests are reviewed

Every request is considered for concept, placement, scale, timing, budget, and whether I can make the work well. A clear idea helps me respond with a useful next step, but submitting it does not guarantee approval or an appointment.

Flash, Custom, Build, and Special Projects begin from different relationships to the work. Choose the closest path; if the project needs more discussion, I may recommend a consultation or ask for additional context before a tattoo date is offered.

Your idea and my interpretation

You bring the subject, meaning, desired feeling, placement idea, and any boundaries that matter. I bring composition, visual language, scale, contrast, and decisions about what will read on the body. Collaboration does not mean that either person disappears from the work.

I do not make exact copies of another artist’s tattoo or repeat a previous custom piece. If an exact image is essential to you, this may not be the right practice. If you are open to an original interpretation, tell me both what must stay and where I have room to explore.

References and designs you bring

A reference can point to a mood, material, story, symbolic relationship, or compositional quality. It is not a production-ready tattoo. You can also name a Tattoo, Tattoo Design, or Legend symbol from the Construct that resonates with your idea.

If you have drawn something yourself—or used a digital or AI image to explore an idea—show what you are responding to, not just a final image you expect me to trace. Anatomy, size, contrast, existing work, and how the image may age can require substantial changes. Build Your Own is a guided brief-making path, not a promise that every selected mark will be used unchanged.

How the design develops

Once a project is approved and booked, I make time for research, references, composition, and preparation. The developed design is normally shown at the tattoo appointment; a separately requested, approved, and paid consultation can create an earlier review opportunity.

The current tattoo deposit includes one developed design direction. Minor refinements to that direction and redraws I initiate remain included. If you request a substantially different alternate concept and I approve it, the current policy calls for a separate non-refundable $50 drawing fee before work on that concept begins. A major last-minute change can require a new appointment and deposit.

Placement, skin, and photographs

A real photograph of your intended body area helps me see its shape, available space, surrounding tattoos, and how a composition might move there. For cover-ups and reworks, clear photographs of the existing tattoo are especially important. Internet examples can explain visual direction, but they cannot stand in for your body.

You do not need to choose a skin-tone category to ask for a tattoo. Color, contrast, scarring, texture, sensitivity, and prior work should be considered individually. Placement and body photographs are private review material; they are not public portfolio permission. The prototype does not accept uploads.

Rates, budgets, and deposits

Current public rate snapshot, reviewed September 16, 2026: Flash $150/hr; Custom $200/hr; Special Projects $100+/hr; Build Your Own quoted after review. Each real Special Project or artist-led offering needs its own reviewed terms. These rates are not a quote for your particular project.

Your inquiry asks what total project budget you are comfortable working within. I review that separately from the project budget presented with an approved session plan. You see and acknowledge that reviewed budget before scheduling through a private booking link.

Current standard tattoo booking deposits are $50 for a Quarter Day, $100 for a Half Day, $150 for a 3/4 Day, $200 for a Full Day, and $350 for an optional Extended Day. Deposits are non-refundable and are credited toward the tattoo. The Extended Day also has a $200 fee and reserves a 12-hour appointment block. These are current terms, not a new prototype offer.

How sessions are planned

Current standard appointment options are 2, 4, 6, and 8 hours, plus an optional 8–12 hour Extended Day. The number and length of sessions depend on the idea, size, detail, body area, your pace, and how the work needs to develop. A ¼ sleeve is not automatically a multi-session tattoo.

For larger work, I plan the whole piece and then recommend a schedule after review. Some projects can be completed section by section across consecutive days in one trip. Others benefit from separate visits, with time to heal before I return to areas for deeper contrast and refinement. Your ability to travel back matters; neither approach is required for every project.

Approved clients review the proposed appointment count, pacing, and project budget before private scheduling. A requested date on the inquiry is context, not a reservation.

Traveling to Atlanta

If you are coming from outside Atlanta, tell me whether you can make a return trip and how many appointment days you could make in one visit. For larger projects, you can tell me whether back-to-back days or separate visits would work better if more than one appointment is needed. This is a preference, not a commitment to a particular method or number of sessions.

Please do not purchase non-refundable travel around a requested date. Wait for an approved project, an agreed session plan, and confirmed appointments. A one-trip finish or a return visit is decided project by project.

Day of, rescheduling, and aftercare

Clients must be at least 18 and bring valid ID. Arrive fed, hydrated, rested, and in clothing that gives access to the tattoo area. The current policy allows a 15-minute grace period; later arrival may cancel the appointment and require a new deposit.

Current policy allows one reschedule with at least 48 hours’ notice. A later request requires a new deposit. At the appointment, we confirm final design, placement, and session price; the remaining balance is due before tattooing begins. Review the live studio policies and the day-of and aftercare resources before attending.

Terms & Conditions

Policy snapshot checked September 17, 2026. These are the current studio terms in plain language. The live Studio & Booking Policies ↗ are the full, authoritative version. Sending an inquiry does not book an appointment; booking confirms that you have read and understood the policies.

  • Age and ID: You must be at least 18 and bring valid ID. There are no exceptions.
  • Design: Exact copies are not offered. Your design is normally shown at the appointment. An earlier consultation must be requested, approved, and paid for.
  • Design changes: A paid tattoo deposit includes one developed design direction. Minor refinements and artist-initiated redraws are included. If an alternate concept you request is approved, a separate, non-refundable $50 drawing fee is due before that work begins; it is not credited toward the tattoo. Major last-minute changes can require a new appointment and deposit.
  • Deposit and rescheduling: Tattoo deposits are non-refundable and credited toward the tattoo. One reschedule is allowed with at least 48 hours’ notice. A request made within 48 hours requires a new deposit.
  • Arrival and no-shows: There is a 15-minute grace period. Arriving more than 15 minutes late cancels the appointment and requires a new deposit to reschedule. A no-show forfeits the deposit and will not be rebooked.
  • Payment: After the final design, placement, and session price are confirmed, the remaining balance is due before tattooing starts. Cash is preferred; Cash App, Apple Pay, and credit/debit cards are also accepted. Digital transactions have a 3% processing fee.
  • Guests and health: Let Saiel know in advance if you plan to bring a guest. Service may be refused if you arrive under the influence or unwell, including an active rash, infection, or open wound near the tattoo area.

Review the full live policy before you book. In this prototype, the Custom inquiry shows what an acknowledgment step could look like, but it does not record an agreement.
