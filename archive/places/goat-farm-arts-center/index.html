01 / Place

Reading the campus

The public name Goat Farm Arts Center belongs to an industrial complex that the National Register records as the E. Van Winkle Gin and Machine Works. The individual property was listed September 10, 1979, and later included within the Howell Interlocking Historic District, listed July 25, 2003.0204

The plant manufactured machinery used to gin, press, clean, bale, and process cotton and cotton seed. It was not a textile mill: cotton was not spun or woven here as the site's principal historic industry.0304

Today the Goat Farm is an operating cultural center with apartments, artist studios, exhibitions, performances, and satellite studio locations. Phase 1 of the current redevelopment opened in 2024, but the wider adaptive-reuse program and MOCA GA's new museum were not fully complete at this dossier's August 2026 research cutoff.011116

02 / Industry

A machine works for cotton

Edward Van Winkle formed his Atlanta company in 1870 after arriving from New Jersey. The Foster Street works, his third Atlanta location, opened in 1889 beside three rail lines. At its peak, the complex contained more than fifteen buildings and ranked among the South's largest cotton-related industrial plants.03

The company made cotton gins, presses, feeders, cleaners, baling systems, shafting, castings, and cotton-seed-oil machinery. The later district study describes it as Atlanta's largest cotton-machinery plant and apparently Georgia's only large-scale producer of cotton-seed-oil processing equipment during the period.04

An 1898 city guidebook presented the plant as part of Atlanta's industrial identity. Its image is valuable evidence of the operating complex, but promotional publishing and later photographic reproduction limit how precisely it can date individual surviving structures.20

Terminology control

Textile-mill language is a recurring secondary shorthand and is excluded here. The primary historic records identify a cotton-machinery factory: an industry supporting cotton agriculture and processing, not a plant spinning fiber into cloth.

03 / Form

An industrial campus, not one building

The 1979 nomination measured an 11.3-acre complex of one-, two-, and three-story red-brick buildings with load-bearing masonry walls and heavy timber-and-plank interiors. Cast iron, wood and corrugated metal, reinforced concrete, and steel framing appear in smaller portions of the site.03

Production yards and buildings formed a cross-axial, hierarchical plan oriented primarily toward the railroad. The central warehouse and manufacturing buildings preserved arched openings, segmental brickwork, clerestories, monitors, heavy timbers, and a ground-level passage joining working yards.03

The 2003 Howell Interlocking nomination explicitly updates portions of the 1979 building chronology after deeper review of Sanborn fire-insurance maps. It establishes the industry on this site in 1889, places the major building campaign between 1889 and 1911, and dates most principal Buildings 5–8 by 1892; Buildings 6 and 7 may date to 1889.04

04 / Succession

The Murray years and wartime production

Around 1912, the Murray Company of Texas acquired Van Winkle's Atlanta operation and changed the plant's name. Historic references therefore alternate among E. Van Winkle Gin and Machine Works, E. Van Winkle Gin & Machinery Co., The Murray Company, Murray Mill, and Murray's Mill.0304

During World War II, the plant produced ammunition and mortars. In the mid-1950s it was sold to David C. Black; after Black's death in 1966, the Trust Company of Georgia held it until Robert S. Haywood purchased the property in 1972.03

By the 1978 preservation survey, Southeastern Metal Products was making boat trailers, sheet metal, cotton-gin parts, and machinery on site. Smaller tenants worked in glass and stained glass, wood products, printing and apparel, lumber, auto repair, and hardened steel. The nomination found no significant historic cotton-ginning equipment remaining.03

Verified wartime description

The 1979 nomination says “ammunition and mortars.” Later descriptions that reduce this history to “artillery” are not used as the controlling account.

05 / Reuse

Goats, workshops, and artists

Published histories credit Haywood with using goats to control kudzu and with renting inexpensive workspaces to craftspeople and artists. Artist Katherine Mitchell recalled moving into the complex in 1982 and believed she was its first artist resident. The story explains the later public name, but it does not establish that goats remain part of the present campus.05

By the 2003 Howell Interlocking survey, residential lofts, small businesses, and art and design studios were already recognized uses. The artist layer preceded the formal Goat Farm Arts Center identity by decades.04

Hallister Property Development LLC, led by Christopher Melhouse and Anthony Harper, signed an agreement in April 2008 allowing it to possess and develop the property. A federal court later recorded that Hallister first tested apartment and retail conversion, built artist studios for cash flow, and maintained the artist community after the recession derailed the broader plan.06

Harper later said the property purchase officially closed in 2010 and that the intentional arts model developed around that time. By 2012, reporting counted 365 artists in more than 150 studios, 25 residential lofts, and roughly ten events a month.0507

Acquisition distinction

“Bought in 2008” collapses two events. The primary court record establishes possession and development rights in 2008; Harper's later account places the completed purchase in 2010. The arts-center model is safest dated to the 2008–2010 transition.

06 / Firsthand

A lived studio thread, 2018–2021

Saiel Dauhn Solehman established a work-only studio at the Goat Farm in 2018. The surviving photographs currently supplied from that first phase were captured on September 24 and November 13, 2019; the phase date and the individual image dates therefore remain distinct.24

Later in 2019, Solehman moved into a live/work studio and remained at the Goat Farm through 2021. Photographs captured January 25, 2020, and May 11, 2021, document that second space. In 2021, as redevelopment reached her occupancy, she moved to the Goat Farm's satellite studios in the J.R. Erikson Co. Building at 364 Nelson Street.24

This is a firsthand record of one artist's passage through work, residence, redevelopment, and relocation. It sits inside the wider place history without being generalized into a claim about every tenant or every part of the campus.

07 / Development

Redevelopment, preservation, and support

In 2019, Goat Farm and TriBridge Residential announced a broad redevelopment intended to preserve the historic campus while adding the density and rental income needed to support its cultural work. Contemporary coverage described a roughly $250 million, half-million-square-foot vision involving housing, studios, venues, food uses, offices, a hotel, and a permanent MOCA GA home. That figure described the wider announced vision; the reviewed records do not establish a $250 million City of Atlanta investment or a completed-project expenditure of that amount.09

The transition displaced the existing community during construction. Artists were expected to leave by the end of October 2019, and reporting documented interim satellite options, promises of return, affordability concerns, and fears that physical redevelopment would dissolve an existing social network.09 That date describes the announced transition rather than proof that every tenant vacated then: Solehman's firsthand archive documents her individual live/work occupancy through 2021 and her later move to the Erikson satellite.24

Public participation took narrower, distinct forms. The Development Authority of Fulton County facilitated the residential-and-studio phase through up to $55 million in taxable revenue bonds and a leasehold tax-incentive structure. Its 2019 estimate projected approximately $2.79 million in property-tax savings over ten years while the project continued paying property tax; the authority's own explanation says the borrower supplies or purchases the bonds and arranges any separate debt or equity. The structure facilitated private financing through a measured tax benefit rather than representing a $55 million City or county expenditure. An Atlanta BeltLine housing report separately lists no ABI/Invest Atlanta investment in the completed apartments and identifies their 32 income-restricted apartments as inclusionary-zoning units.081023

The City of Atlanta's clearest documented connection came later through a distinct cultural institution. MOCA GA's official groundbreaking record says the City announced a $2 million contribution to the museum's separate capital campaign in July 2024. The source does not identify the appropriation or payment mechanism, so this dossier records an announced contribution—not a City investment in the entire Goat Farm redevelopment.16

Construction and development extended well beyond the original schedule. Phase 1 ultimately opened in spring 2024 with 209 apartments, ground-level artist spaces, public courtyards, and residential amenities. Active programming returned, while adaptive reuse of historic interiors and MOCA GA's museum continued beyond that opening.11121416

08 / Control

A campus divided across legal entities

Ownership cannot be reduced to one campus-wide name. A January 2022 Development Authority resolution records a $55 million Goat Farm Development bond structure, an October 2021 lease to RB Preston Ridge Co-Tenant LLC, and approval to assign the leasehold and bond interests to TBR 1200 Foster Street Owner LLC or an affiliate.10

Fulton County's 2026 assessor layer identifies seven clearly campus-related parcels totaling approximately 11.74 acres. They are divided among the Development Authority of Fulton County, the Museum of Contemporary Art of Georgia, Deneb Goat Farm LLC, GF Dovetail Equity LLC, GF Goodson Owner LLC, and GF B5 Owner LLC.17

The Development Authority's assessor title should not be mistaken for arts or apartment operation: the bond documents establish a separate leasehold structure. MOCA GA owns its 1.05-acre museum parcel after closing its purchase in December 2023.1016

09 / Present

Active, still becoming

The Goat Farm's official calendar documents an active 2026 campus: exhibitions, workshops, record and print programs, concerts, Warhorse summer programming, and SITE 2026. The October festival is presented as the third annual campus-wide exhibition, with more than fifty artists across twelve acres.01

TriBridge records the completed first phase as 209 apartments and 45 artist studios. Niles Bolton reports 48 studios, the AJC reports 51 studios, and ArtsATL described more than 50 studios. These published studio counts differ and remain unresolved, likely because the sources use different definitions or scopes.111214

The completed phase includes 32 apartments restricted to households at or below 80 percent of area median income, fewer than the 36 proposed in 2019. Atlanta's current workforce-housing list records the property at 80 percent AMI, accepting Housing Choice vouchers and not using LIHTC.1113

MOCA GA's official current page describes a 26,306-square-foot future home with construction underway. The museum broke ground in December 2024; a May 2026 construction tour reported a hoped-for summer opening. No confirmed opening was found by August 29. The safest status is under construction, not verified open.16

11 / Research

Open questions

  • 01

    When was the water tower constructed?

    Historic research proves it existed by 1931 and may have existed by 1911. Later redevelopment material states 1911 without resolving that uncertainty.

  • 02

    What is the complete deed and beneficial-ownership chain?

    Current assessor parcels and public bond documents establish multiple legal layers, but they are not a complete title examination.

  • 03

    How many completed artist studios should be counted?

    Credible sources publish 45, 48, 51, and “more than 50.” A scope-by-scope inventory is still needed.

  • 04

    Which historic-building interiors are complete?

    The 2024 plan anticipated a later adaptive-reuse phase, but no current official inventory confirms completion building by building.

  • 05

    Which 2019 master-plan elements remain active?

    The current disposition of the announced hotel, offices, restaurants, café, bookshop, and every proposed venue has not been verified.

  • 06

    When will MOCA GA officially open at the Goat Farm?

    A May 2026 report described a hoped-for summer opening, while the museum's current page still calls the facility a future home under construction.

  • 07

    Does any local historic designation protect the campus?

    The federal individual and district listings are verified. No City of Atlanta landmark designation was located in this pass.
