01 / Place

Reading the place

The painted J. R. Erikson Co., Inc. name preserves one long chapter of this building’s life, not its origin. Historic mapping does not show the present warehouse on the block in 1911, while preservation records place most of Castleberry Hill’s surviving Nelson and Walker Street warehouses in the 1920s.0103

By the late 1950s, J. Roscoe Erikson’s upholstery-supply business was operating at 364 Nelson. Beginning in 2012, MASS Collective brought shared fabrication and makerspace activity into the building; in 2013, Erikson Clock emerged as a satellite arts venue.050910

MASS Collective is now a former tenant and is based at Lifecycle Building Center on Murphy Avenue. The Nelson Street building continues as a working multi-tenant property containing creative studios and entertainment uses, including FamFam and art.pill TATTOO HOUSE.1213

02 / Before

Before the warehouse

The 1911 Sanborn sheets show a different Nelson and Mangum landscape: wood-frame dwellings, smaller neighborhood uses, rail infrastructure, and open ground appear where later warehouse blocks would take form. The present multi-story brick building is not shown.01

Atlanta’s 1922 city directory still records residents and small businesses along this stretch of Nelson Street, including a grocery, barber, and lunch counter.02 Historic street numbering has not yet been matched conclusively to the present parcel, so these records describe the block without claiming that a particular household or shop occupied the exact footprint of today’s building.

03 / Context

Rail remakes the block

Castleberry Hill’s warehouse landscape grew from Atlanta’s role as a railroad and regional distribution center. A Southern Railway spur constructed through this area between 1913 and 1915 changed the shape and use of the Nelson–Walker blocks.03

The National Register nomination describes rows of two- and three-story warehouses constructed principally during the 1920s and states that nearly all the surviving warehouses in this stretch were present by 1927. That district evidence makes a 1920s date plausible for the present building, but it is not a substitute for its permit, deed, or tax record.03

The transformation was not socially neutral. City and preservation histories document the loss of residential fabric, including parts of Castleberry Hill’s Black residential community, during railroad and warehouse expansion. The evidence supports that district history; it does not yet establish that this particular warehouse displaced a named household.04

Contextual inference

The safest construction description is “likely associated with Castleberry Hill’s 1920s warehouse buildout; exact year unresolved.” The commercial real-estate date of 1910 conflicts with the 1911 map and is excluded from this dossier.16

04 / Industry

The Erikson era

A June 1957 Atlanta telephone directory lists Erikson Company at 364 Nelson Street, and the city directory identifies J. Roscoe Erikson and the business’s wholesale upholstery-supply trade.05 A national trade advertisement still used 228 Mitchell Street that summer, while trade literature used 364 Nelson by October 1958. The overlap most likely records a move or delayed advertising update, but the exact transition date remains unresolved.06

By 1961, an interiors-industry directory listed J.R. Erikson Co., Inc. at 364 Nelson. The company supplied upholstery fabrics and related materials; it was not a general heavy manufacturer.06

Atlanta’s 1999 neighborhood field survey found the supply company still operating here with eleven administrative, sales, warehouse, delivery, and machine-operation positions. The facade lettering therefore records a long period of real work at the address, though not the building’s original identity.04

05 / Reuse

Fabrication and performance

A June 2012 event announcement places the newly formed MASS Collective at 364 Nelson. Shared tools, fabrication equipment, prototyping, teaching, and a community of builders extended the building’s production-oriented use into a new era.09

Published reporting dates the launch of Goat Farm’s Erikson Clock satellite venue to 2013. Contemporary program records document experimental music, theater, printmaking, exhibitions, workshops, literary gatherings, and interdisciplinary events inside the warehouse.1011

The precise origin of the word “Clock,” the end date of formal Erikson Clock programming, and the property’s full ownership history remain unverified.

06 / Present

A working building

Today, 364 Nelson remains a mixed-use creative and entertainment property rather than a single institution. The brick warehouse form and painted Erikson name remain visible while studios and public-facing businesses continue to work inside it.

By 2026, MASS Collective no longer occupied 364 Nelson, and MASS and Lifecycle identified 1116 Murphy Avenue SW as the makerspace’s current location. Its exact final operating day on Nelson Street has not been independently documented.12

FamFam currently lists 364 Nelson as its address, and art.pill TATTOO HOUSE works within the building. Appointment visitors should use the separate location and parking resource for current arrival guidance.13

08 / Research

Open questions

  • 01

    When was this specific warehouse constructed?

    The 1910 commercial-listing date conflicts with the 1911 map. A permit card, tax digest, deed sequence, or later Sanborn correction is still needed.16

  • 02

    Who commissioned, designed, and built it?

    No original developer, architect, contractor, or first tenant has been verified.

  • 03

    How did the block’s street numbers change?

    Historic directories and a 1933 residential use of “364 Nelson” complicate a direct match to the present parcel.15

  • 04

    What is the complete ownership chain?

    Published reporting describes Goat Farm involvement, but the deed history and current legal ownership have not been independently established.

  • 05

    Why was the arts venue called Erikson Clock?

    No dependable source located so far explains the addition of “Clock.”

  • 06

    When did each reuse phase end?

    The final dates of formal Erikson Clock programming and MASS Collective’s last operating day at 364 Nelson remain unresolved.
