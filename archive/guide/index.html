01 / Navigation

Ways into the Archive

There is no single required path. Search for something specific, refine the public index, or follow relationships from one record into another.

Search

Use the whole public record.

Search can match titles, stories, material captions, transcripts, people, relationships, and dated history—not only the name of a work.

Refine

Narrow the field.

Filters organize published records by record type, material type, medium, brand, person, era, and collection.

Follow

Move through connections.

Open a record, then follow its subjects, collections, origin thread, history, or connected works to continue through the system.

02 / Structure

Anatomy of a record

A record is the stable public identity of a work, person, place, event, symbol, product, or other part of the Construct. Its documentation can grow without creating a second identity for the same thing.

Cultural object

Paintings, drawings, garments, tattoo designs, individual tattoo executions, books, recordings, and other authored artifacts may be catalogued as cultural objects. The dossier documents a cultural object; an uploaded file represents or documents it. People, organizations, places, concepts, and activities remain related entities rather than being mislabeled as objects.

  1. 01
    Overview

    The identifying title, orientation, date, medium, status, and primary image.

  2. 02
    Story

    The public context of the record, along with subjects and collections that help locate it.

  3. 03
    Evolution

    Published versions and Roman-numeral states, each represented by its reviewed lead material and stable shareable anchor.

  4. 04
    Catalogue documentation

    Applicable public facts grouped by identity, physical object, production, remarks, references, institutional history, and rights.

  5. 05
    Open notebook

    Published process evidence: images, videos, notes, audio, sketches, documents, and artifacts assigned to the state they document.

  6. 06
    History

    Dated or honestly undated activities that show how the record has changed over time.

  7. 07
    Connections

    Same-medium works, cross-medium Construct relationships, origin threads, and contextual or provenance entities.

03 / Evidence

Materials and documentation

Materials document a record; they do not replace it. Several photographs, videos, and notes can belong to the same work and the same moment in its development. A material describes the evidence; its optional Digital asset is the uploaded file that carries it.

Three documentary roles

State materials are direct evidence attached to one creative condition. Source-material sets preserve an ordered exchange or group of references that informed one or more states. Catalogue documentation records applicable facts about the cultural object as a whole. Canonical title, date, medium, artist, organizations, production roles, state changes, and Digital-asset visibility remain authoritative in their existing records instead of being copied into documentation entries.

Digital asset
The uploaded image, audio, video, or document file representing or documenting a material. It retains separate privacy, public-presentation, and transcript controls.
Final image
A primary view of the completed or currently resolved work.
Process photograph
A documented stage, action, studio condition, or visual change.
Sketch
A drawing, study, plan, or compositional test connected to the record.
Video
Moving-image evidence of making, installation, use, performance, or context.
Note
Visitor-facing inline text that can exist without an uploaded file.
Voice memo
Recorded reflection or oral documentation, with a transcript when available.
Document
A PDF or other written source that belongs with the record.
Artifact
A physical remainder, reference object, tool, or other piece of material evidence.
Source material
Evidence supplied, exchanged, or consulted during development. A source set belongs to the dossier and may document more than one creative state.
Client correspondence
One ordered source-material set containing scrubbed correspondence pages, pasted text, documents, and client-supplied reference photographs. Public attribution is always “Client.”
Adaptive documentation

Only applicable groups appear.

Public entries are grouped under identity, physical object, production, remarks, references, institutional history, and rights. Empty groups and internal entries are omitted from the public dossier.

Repeatable entries

One field can hold several sources.

Alternate titles, inscriptions, bibliography, remarks, identifiers, collections, and rights notes may repeat. Each entry keeps its own order, citation or URL, and public visibility.

Client source workflow

Draft first, publish deliberately.

A new correspondence set begins as internal and draft. Its ordered entries may be individually excluded from public view. Publishing requires a public dossier, at least one linked public state, and eligible included Digital assets.

Redacted-upload assumption

Only scrubbed evidence is uploaded.

Correspondence and reference files are assumed to have all personal information removed, faces blurred where needed, and publication clearance completed before upload. The Archive does not retain an unredacted original or run an automated PII check, and original filenames are never shown publicly.

Blackboard lens

One complete state, many contextual details.

Each complete captured blackboard is its own OBJ record. Close-up sketches and ideas remain attached to the paintings or records they informed, appear once by Digital asset, and link back to every public context. A fragment does not need a complete-board match to remain valid evidence.

Scan preservation

The master stays private.

A complete board requires a paired archival master and prepared web derivative. The internal master is available only in authenticated Studio; public pages and APIs expose only the eligible JPEG, PNG, or WebP derivative.

Catalogue documentation vocabulary
Identity
Alternate titles that supplement, but never replace, the canonical title.
Physical object
Object description, technique, support, dimensions, and inscriptions.
Production
Edition, edition and production information, and background.
Remarks
Artist, installation, curatorial, and other remarks.
References
Bibliography and related source citations.
Institutional history
Former catalogue numbers, institutional identifiers, credit line, and other collections.
Rights
Rights, reproduction, and image-permission notes.
04 / Catalogue

The catalogue key

Live system · reviewed record by record

Assigned prefixes and base numbers are permanent. Classification may become more precise only when its catalogue prefix remains unchanged. Versions, states, materials, and documentation can grow without renumbering the cultural object.

ART-004
Permanent object identityThe stable base catalogue number of Lost Marbles. It is never renumbered when documentation grows.
.1
VersionA materially distinct realization, restart, support, or branch of the composition.
/I
StateA meaningful documented condition within the same version. States use Roman numerals.
variant 1
VariantA related alternative when it is more honest not to claim a strict sequence.
Current public condition

A pointer to one real public state.

The current marker is selected from a published public version and state. It is not a manually duplicated label. The selected state keeps older catalogue fields synchronized for compatibility.

State lead

One confirmed image or video.

Every reviewed state selects a lead material belonging to that state. A public lead must also be a published public material whose Digital asset is active, public, and shown inline.

Important distinction

A process photograph is not automatically a new state. Materials attach to the state they document. The Roman numeral advances only when the work itself has undergone a meaningful documented change.

Version

A substantial re-realization.

A version records a restart, new support, reformatting, construction change, or other material branch that retains the cultural object’s core identity.

State

A condition within a version.

A state records a meaningful documented change inside one version. Its name follows the actual medium: a painting condition, Merch prototype, tattoo drawing revision, or tattoo session.

Stable state links

Every public state has a stable anchor inside its canonical dossier. The state remains part of the record rather than becoming a separate page, so shared links preserve both the permanent object identity and the exact documented condition.

05 / Lineage

Relationships and origin threads

The Archive treats connection as evidence. A painting can become a source for a print, garment, tattoo, writing, or event without those later forms becoming the same record.

Identity and lineage

The catalogue number identifies the cultural object. Relationships connect independently catalogued objects across the ecosystem. A Merch object does not place its source painting’s number inside its own identity, and a tattoo execution remains distinct from the design it executes.

Relationship

A named connection.

Relationships describe how two canonical records affect, reference, derive from, contain, document, or otherwise belong to one another.

Origin thread

A shared inception.

An origin thread gathers source records, related outcomes, and public process evidence around the same point of origin.

Archive dossier
The one-to-one Archive record that documents and publishes one canonical cultural object or Event without replacing its identity.
Person
An artist, wearer, collaborator, printer, photographer, or other person connected by a named role.
Organization
Six.Well, a manufacturer, gallery, publisher, printer, retailer, or other organization.
Place
A studio, venue, exhibition location, production location, or tattoo location.
Event or activity
An exhibition, tattoo session, production run, publication, installation, release, or other milestone.
Concept or theme
A reusable controlled idea such as Lost Marbles, transformation, or memory.
Relationship

06 / Comparison

Comparing public records and states

Comparison is a two-subject reading tool. A subject can be a whole cultural-object record or one specific public state, allowing state-to-state, work-to-work, and cross-medium comparisons without creating duplicate records.

Comparison workspace

Choose a left and right subject.

Use the Compare records action at the top of the Archive explorer. The comparison workspace provides one public-record selector for each side; individual record, state, and related-work cards do not carry comparison buttons.

Shareable source

The URL records the reading.

The comparison page reads its subjects from the URL. Choosing two records builds that URL, which remains the authority when a comparison is shared.

/archive/compare/
?left=lostmarbles
&left_state=<optional-public-state-id>
&right=lostmarbles-hoodie
&right_state=<optional-public-state-id>
Lead media
The selected public image or video representing each compared record or state.
Identity
Catalogue number, title, date, medium, and cultural-object type.
Physical documentation
Description, technique, support, dimensions, inscriptions, edition, and credit line when documented.
Context
Current or selected state information, themes, and named relationships.
Undocumented value
An em dash keeps the row visible when one subject has information and the other does not.
Responsive reading
Subjects remain side by side on larger screens and stack into a left/right reading on mobile.
Evidence threshold

State comparison appears only when a record has at least two evidence-backed public states. Lost Marbles remains a single-state public record until authentic process evidence, chronology, captions, dates, visibility, and lead media have been reviewed.

07 / Access

What becomes public

The catalogue meaning of a work and the publishing state of its webpage are separate systems. “State III” describes creative evolution; “published” describes access.

LayerWhat it describesTypical values
Creative stateThe work's documented evolutionI, II, III, variant
Record publicationWhether the dossier is releasedDraft, published, archived
Version publicationWhether a version participates in public evolutionDraft, published, archived + public/internal
State publicationWhether a state and its stable anchor are publicDraft, published, archived + public/internal
Material visibilityWho may encounter a materialPublic, unlisted, internal, private
Source-material publicationWhether one grouped source set and its included entries appear publiclyDraft, published, archived + public/internal
Documentation visibilityWhether one catalogue entry appears publiclyPublic, internal
PresentationWhether eligible media appears on the pageInline, hidden
Public media rule

For a cultural object, an uploaded file appears publicly only when its record, version, state, and material are published and public; the material belongs to that state; its presentation is inline; and the Digital asset is active and public. Inline note text is visible when the note material itself is public, published, and assigned to a public state. Contextual Event records are exempt from state assignment because events do not have creative states.

Public client correspondence rule

A client correspondence set is public only after an explicit publication action. The dossier and canonical record must be public, at least one linked version and state must be published and public, and every included Digital asset must be active, public, and inline. Internal state links and individually excluded entries remain absent from public output.

Internal means absent

Internal versions, states, documentation entries, materials, captions, transcripts, relationships, and Digital assets are omitted from public APIs, search results, comparison responses, and public pages. Publication at one layer never overrides a private layer beneath it.

08 / Language

Working glossary

Canonical record
The single stable identity used across the Construct for one work or subject.
Cultural object
An authored work or artifact with its own catalogue identity, history, and relationships.
Dossier
The archive-facing documentation attached to a canonical record.
Composition
The conceptual and visual work family represented by the base catalogue number.
Version
A materially distinct realization or branch of a composition.
State
A meaningful documented stage within a version.
Variant
A related alternative that should not be forced into a false chronology.
Current public condition
The selected published public state used for the record's current catalogue label and marker.
Lead material
The confirmed image or video chosen to represent one documented state.
State anchor
The stable shareable fragment that opens a public state inside its canonical dossier.
Catalogue documentation
Ordered, repeatable object facts that augment authoritative identity, production, state, visibility, and rights data.
Material
An image, video, note, audio file, sketch, document, or artifact that documents a record.
Source material
An ordered body of evidence supplied, exchanged, or consulted during development and linked to one or more states.
Client correspondence set
A scrubbed sequence of correspondence, documents, pasted text, and client reference photographs attributed publicly as “Client.”
Sample
An optional Merch prototype or physical sample documented inside a product’s evolution.
Activity
A dated or honestly undated event in the life of a record.
Collection
A curated grouping built around a body of work, period, place, or question.
Facet
A controlled term used to filter or discover records.
Relationship
A named connection between two canonical records.
Origin thread
A curated lineage joining records and evidence that share an inception.
Comparison subject
A public record or one specific public state occupying the left or right comparison slot.
