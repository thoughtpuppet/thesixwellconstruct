what it does

Purpose

The construct turns experience into artifacts, artifacts into worlds, worlds into community, and community into a structure that can hold its own weight. It is a system for making meaning tangible — a small universe, under construction.

plane I — what the world runs on

The Deep

Below the surface, the world runs on containers. A container is anything that gives experience a boundary, a context, and a meaning. Without containers, ideas become fog. The construct exists to give form to the fog.

containers

What a container is

A tattoo contains a story in skin. A room contains a state of being. A garment contains an edition, a symbol, a moment. A letter contains a dispatch from inside the system. Each one is a small vessel for something that would otherwise drift.

The chain of containers

A painting contains a thoughtform. A business contains a ritualized exchange. A building contains the future institution. The construct contains the bodies. The founder contains the original logic. It is the container of containers — the universe the vessels float in.

the system is alive

Not a dead object

The construct is closer to a living system than a brand. It has bodies, rituals, artifacts, archives, symbols, laws, seasons, transmissions, public doors, hidden rooms, and future expansions. It does not ask only how to make money. It asks how a world functions.

Evidence over theory

A world is real only if it leaves evidence: objects, images, writing, records, events, transactions, archives, rituals, work. No evidence, no world.

products versus artifacts

Products fall out of orbit

Products are bought. They solve a problem, burn up in the feed, and are gone. A shirt with a logo is merch. Most of the market stops there.

Artifacts stay

Artifacts are collected. They carry meaning: limited editions, numbered releases, story-linked objects, physical markings, archive records, ritualized drops. A numbered garment tied to a myth, an event, a symbol, or a project holds its orbit. Each object should feel like it fell from the world.

plane II — the shapes the world navigates by

The Constellations

The construct navigates by six recurring shapes. They are meant to be felt before they are explained — the symbols need oxygen, and the sky needs dark.

Wells

Depth, extraction, hidden knowledge, descent, retrieval. A well is a container that reaches downward. Meaning is not floating on the surface; you have to draw it up. This is the foundation of the name — and of this page.

Rooms

States of being, thresholds, interior realities, designed experiences. A room is not just architecture. A room is a condition — it can heal, trap, reveal, restore, or transform.

Mazes

Searching, uncertainty, intelligence, navigation, transformation through movement. A maze is not confusion. A maze is a process.

Doors

Access, initiation, permission, transition. The site itself works like a door system: some doors are public, some are hidden, some open later. Not everything needs to be visible at once.

Containers

The master symbol. Bodies, rooms, websites, garments, films, paintings, rituals, businesses, archives, buildings. The construct itself is the container of containers.

Artifacts

The physical evidence of the world: tattoos, garments, prints, books, zines, films, letters, event objects, numbered editions, ritual documents, archives. The artifact is how the abstract becomes touchable.

plane III — the bodies of the system

The Orbit

Every branch of the system is a body with its own function, output, audience, and economic role. The bodies do not all orbit at the same distance. Some are close and hot. Some are still forming. Some are far and quiet. One is beyond the visible.

orbital states

Close orbit — active

Currently operating or directly needed. At any given time only a few bodies hold close orbit. Everything else is documented, not pursued.

Forming — incubating

Defined but not fully built. Gathering mass behind the public surface.

Far orbit — dormant

Important later, not a current priority. A seed is not a failure because it is not a forest yet.

Beyond the visible — mythic

Not businesses yet, but they shape the world and pull the system forward, the way unseen mass bends light.

plane IV — the first body

The Origin

Every system has a first body. Sai Solehman is a surrealist tattoo artist, painter, filmmaker, and founder building the six.well construct: an artist-led ecosystem where symbols become tattoos, garments, films, rituals, and spaces.

the founder thesis

An artist who builds containers

The work does not stop at making objects. A tattoo becomes a ritual. A hoodie becomes an artifact. A website becomes a map. A letter becomes a dispatch. A studio becomes a temple-workshop. A business becomes a body in orbit. A life becomes architecture. That is the signature.

The role, in stages

Now: founder-operator. Next: creative director and systems builder. Eventually: architect and steward. The job is not to push every rock forever — it is to define the terrain, build the first roads, and decide what is allowed to grow there.

the binary star
  • The artist wants mystery.
  • The architect wants structure.
  • The entrepreneur wants cash.
  • The philosopher wants meaning.
  • The filmmaker wants narrative.
  • The tattooist wants clients.
  • The institution-builder wants legacy.

All of them are real

Seven selves sharing one sky. The construct is the attempt to stop forcing them to collide and give each one an orbit instead. Not killing parts of the self — sequencing them, so every part stops grabbing the steering wheel at the same time.

working instincts

Symbolic intelligence

Reading the world through symbols, patterns, myth, and hidden structure. The work means something — the craft is building the doorway into that meaning.

Systems thinking

Tattooing connects to clothing, clothing to film, film to events, events to community, community to physical space, space to legacy. Ecosystems, not isolated products.

Long-range vision

Thinking in years and decades — an annual ritual, a future compound, a cultural institution in embryo — while executing in weeks. That is the bridge.

plane V — the constants

The Fixed Stars

Everything else in the system moves. These do not. Seven laws and one governing doctrine that the construct navigates by — they keep the mythology honest and the system buildable.

Build doors before secret rooms.

The governing rule of execution. The construct can have hidden depth, but hidden depth cannot be the only entrance. Simple door. Deep room.

Law one

Every node must serve the whole

If a body does not feed revenue, audience, meaning, archive, or community, it must be questioned.

Law two

Every artifact must carry memory

Products should have context. Drops should have records. Events should leave traces. Work should not vanish into the feed.

Law three

The public needs doors

No matter how deep the system gets, every public-facing branch needs a clear entrance.

Law four

The founder cannot be the only infrastructure

If only one person can understand it, run it, explain it, or fix it, the system is fragile.

Law five

Build small versions of large dreams

The compound begins as a room. The archive begins as a folder. The festival begins as a small gathering. The mythology begins as one repeated symbol.

Law six

Cash is not the mission, but it is the oxygen

Do not worship money. Do not ignore it either. No cash, no cathedral.

Law seven

The construct must leave evidence

If it is real, it produces objects, images, writing, records, events, transactions, archives, rituals, and work.

The filter

Five questions before anything begins

Does this create revenue? Grow audience? Produce an artifact? Strengthen the archive? Support the next flagship move? No to all five — it is a distraction. Beautiful, maybe. Still a distraction.

plane VI — the year completing its orbit

The Cycle

Once a year, the world completes an orbit. Solehman's New Year is the annual showcase of the construct — four days in October, the week before the founder's birthday, when every body in the system becomes visible from one room. Most brands have launches. Institutions have rituals.

the shape of the cycle
Thursday

The mark, made in public. The construct touching skin in real time.

Friday

Fashion show

The artifacts, worn. Garments and editions presented as objects from the world.

Saturday

Art show + screening

Paintings on walls, moving image in the room — the visual mythology and its behind-the-scenes record.

Sunday

Artist talk

The language layer. The founder speaking from inside the system, in person.

why it matters

It gathers everything

Tattoos, paintings, garments, films, performances, letters, audience, collectors, collaborators, documentation — the annual proof that the construct is alive.

Recurrence over scale

The first version can be one room, one capsule, one painting wall, one projection, one talk. A strong small ritual beats a giant imaginary festival. Do it every year. Let the myth accrete, the way worlds do — one pass at a time.
