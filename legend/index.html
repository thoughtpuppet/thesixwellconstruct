How to read it

One identity. Many lives.

A symbol begins with a stable core, but it does not end there. Its direction, neighbors, material, placement, and history can shift its charge. The Legend keeps those layers related without flattening them into one permanent definition.

01

Canonical mark

The simplest recognizable form and the stable identity supplied to other systems.

02

Application

How pairing, inversion, direction, enclosure, placement, or scale changes the reading.

03

Visual variant

Flat, dimensional, colored, carved, animated, maze-bound, and material translations.

04

Trace

Where it has lived across tattoos, paintings, garments, films, writings, objects, and rooms.

Where meaning comes from

No symbol arrives empty.

Some meanings are carried through religion, culture, politics, family, or common use. Others come from my own experience. Sometimes I inherit a meaning; sometimes I expand, invert, contest, detach, or combine it. The Legend names those relationships without treating any reading as universal or final.

01

Cultural or inherited

Associations received through histories, communities, institutions, belief systems, family, and shared use.

02

Personal or lived

The meanings formed through my own experience, relationships, memory, practice, and changing understanding.

03

Reoriented

A deliberate expansion, inversion, contesting, detachment, or combination of an inherited reading.

These lenses may overlap. They describe the source of a meaning, while Maze, Figural, and Ritual describe the kind of symbolic form being recorded.

Shared source

The key feeds the construct.

Legend records are reusable source material. A published edit can clarify symbol selection, connect works across mediums, enrich search and archive memory, and keep the same symbolic identity coherent wherever it appears.
