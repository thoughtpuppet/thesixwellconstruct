Mode, Structure, and Development

The Gatherings.

Every other month, each session introduces a distinct theme through which to explore the larger question: How do we carry, store, process and navigate meaning? We invite participants to explore personal history, meaning, and internal associations through varying lenses, then draft visual representations that may become tattoos.

Modes of material collection.

Through interviews, conversation, and prompts, the series helps participants extract and collect these associations as material for translation. Objects, photographs, and stories offer additional points of entry, but they are not prerequisites. The process can begin with an attachment, a response, or a recurring image. Together, participants lay this material out and navigate its relationships to inform the development of a visual vocabulary.

Thinking with others.

Collaboration is part of how this project deepens. Other thinkers bring their own questions, methods, and experiences, helping shape what each gathering explores.

Oral history and memory work as a foundation.

The first edition is being developed in collaboration with Georgia Dusk and its co-founders, Dartricia Rollins and Ashby Combahee, as oral historians and memory workers. Their practices bring distinct approaches to asking and listening into the inquiry, with particular attention to context. Their perspectives help shape the gathering’s questions and the ways participants engage with one another’s material.

A sequence of portable explorations.

The editions of KINMARKING form a developing sequence, each building on what emerges across the series. The first establishes a foundation through the extraction and collection of material and associations. Upcoming editions utilize that material to develop a visual vocabulary through color, iconography, symbolism, and composition. Participants can move between editions through different points of entry and return, revisiting earlier material as new associations and questions emerge.

Future conversations could deepen the inquiry into embodied knowledge, memory, ritual, inheritance, and grief, alongside thinkers working in neuroscience, anthropology, history, and other fields. Each conversation can introduce another way of thinking about interpretation, meaning, preservation, and semiotic endurance.

Each edition is intended to be portable, offering questions, approaches, and exercises that participants can engage with and use beyond the original gathering. Participants can revisit earlier editions, work through ones they missed, and develop material to bring into later editions. Details on how to work through an edition will be available on its page.
