participant guide

Bring a history into conversation.

The participating archivist and tattoo artist help distinguish what is known, remembered, inherited, uncertain, and possible to carry forward.

What to bring

  • Bring one to three photographs, scans, documents, objects, stories, inherited symbols, or other references.
  • Identify what is known and what remains uncertain.
  • Leave irreplaceable originals at home when a photograph or scan will work.
  • Consider permission and privacy when the material involves other people.

How it works

No finished tattoo design is required. Begin with conversation: consider the material with an archivist, then explore with the tattoo artist how—or whether—it may become a mark in the skin.

Possible outcomes

Participation does not require getting tattooed. Small, resolved tattoos may be possible that day as time and technical readiness allow. Developing work may move into a future appointment secured by a separate tattoo deposit.

Same-day readiness

Same-day tattooing is not guaranteed. Anyone hoping for possible same-day work should eat beforehand, hydrate, arrive sober, and bring government-issued photo identification.

Privacy + consent

Materials, conversations, photographs, scans, recordings, and tattoo images are not retained or published without separate consent. Bringing material does not place it in the public Archive.

RSVP + walk-ins

RSVP is optional and provides event correspondence, calendar details, and a reminder. Walk-ins remain welcome. An RSVP is not a tattoo appointment and does not reserve same-day tattoo time.
