What is Signal & Symbol?

Signal & Symbol is a guided creative gathering where participants respond to sensory prompts through drawing, color, line, symbol, and mark-making.

A signal is the prompt placed in the room. A symbol is the visual response that rises from it. The horizon is what each person brings to that encounter: memory, mood, imagination, culture, attention, and the atmosphere already living in them.

Prompts may include short readings, music, textures, tastes, objects, or other sensory experiences. Each person receives the same prompt, but what appears in the mind may be completely different.

The goal is not to draw well. The goal is to respond honestly.

session shape

How the Session Works

  1. 01Arrive and choose simple drawing materials.
  2. 02Receive a sensory prompt.
  3. 03Pause, notice what appears, and create a visual response.
  4. 04Repeat with several prompts.
  5. 05Optionally share what showed up for you.
  6. 06Leave with your response pages, unless you choose to contribute them to the archive.
dry materials

Materials Provided

Drawing tools

Markers, colored pencils, graphite pencils, erasers, and sharpeners.

Surfaces

Paper, drawing boards, or table surfaces for keeping the session simple and easy to manage.

No paint will be used. This is a dry-material session designed to stay simple, accessible, and low-pressure.

who it is for

Who This Is For

This gathering is for curious people, artists, non-artists, writers, thinkers, listeners, overthinkers, quiet observers, and anyone interested in how the mind turns experience into image.

You do not need to know how to draw. You only need to be willing to notice what comes up.

clear edges

What It Is Not

Signal & Symbol is not a drawing class, paint night, critique session, therapy session, or stage for proving anything.

You will not be asked to make something impressive. You will not be graded, corrected, or told what your work means.

optional record

The Archive

Some visual responses may be photographed or documented as part of the ongoing Six.Well Construct archive. Participation in documentation is optional. Guests may opt out, remain anonymous, or decide after the session.
